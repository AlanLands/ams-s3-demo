The scenario

S3 · Enhancement delivery

A small, ordinary change to an application that is already live — the kind a team picks up most weeks. We follow one end to end: a written request on a board, out to a released change.

Five stages, and a person signs off at every one. This is not a slide — the applications are running, and the change is really made to their code.

The estate

MapleSure Insurance

A made-up group benefits insurer. Every application, contract, plan sponsor and person here is invented — no client code and no client data anywhere.

What is not invented is the shape: applications owned by different teams, with the same hand-offs you have. Most of what goes wrong in a change like this goes wrong between applications, not inside one.

The application

EnrolDirect

MapleSure's online enrolment channel. Its user is the plan member — an employee joining a benefit plan themselves, on the web, instead of phoning the service centre.

Every one of them meets the same question: is this person allowed to enrol online? EnrolDirect does not own that rule — the sponsor agrees it, PolicyCore stores it, EnrolDirect enforces it, and three other systems read the answer.

Nothing here is a technical decision. A plan sponsor's rule, enforced by one application and read by three others. What follows is what happens when that rule has to cover a group of people nobody ever wrote a rule for.

What AI is not allowed to do

  • Touch the older tests. People wrote them. They are the independent check that nothing else broke — so AI cannot edit them to agree with itself.
  • Mark its own homework. Whether the change is good to go is read from the job's own history, not from anything AI claims.
  • Put anything live on its own. Everything sits on a separate copy until a person approves the release.
  • Automate everything. Some tests still have to be done by hand — mainframe and other systems AI cannot reach. Those are listed as manual, not hidden.
